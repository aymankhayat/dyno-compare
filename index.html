18 → 9 cars, 45/45 figures sourced

Zero blanks without guessing

The first lineup had 18 US-market cars, but most makers don’t publish top speed and several don’t publish 0–60. Instead of filling gaps with simulated numbers, the lineup was cut to cars whose makers publish all five figures, and the test suite now fails on any blank.

8.90 lb/hp vs Toyota’s published 8.89

Conversions checked against the maker’s own math

All data is stored in published US units and converted in one module. Tests pin the reference values (60 mph = 96.6 km/h, 300 hp = 224 kW) and cross-check derived figures against numbers Toyota prints itself, like the GR Supra’s weight-to-power ratio.

6 models in 19 KB of code, 0 model files

Cars modeled in code, to scale

The first 3D pass extruded generic body styles and looked nothing like the real cars. Each model is now a traced silhouette sized from published length, width, height and wheelbase. One honest gap: Porsche’s US site lists no 911 height, so those two models use a third-party figure, noted in the code.

0 px horizontal overflow at 375 px

A 3D stage that doesn’t trap your thumb

A full-height WebGL canvas swallowed touch scrolling on phones, so rotation is opt-in on touch screens. The render loop pauses off-screen, and the gauge needle sweep moved from requestAnimationFrame to timers after it stalled in background tabs.

Designed & built by Ayman Khayat

I’m Ayman Khayat, a mechanical engineering student. During an internship at a Toyota dealership I worked on hybrid powertrain diagnostics, which is where the hybrid write-up on this page comes from: regenerative braking, e-CVT behavior and battery management are what you see from the service bay, not from the brochure.

Spec sheets tell you what a car makes. They rarely tell you why two cars with similar numbers drive so differently. I built this to put the numbers next to the reasons.

What it demonstrates

  • Data integrity: every figure links to the manufacturer document it came from, and the lineup only includes cars whose makers publish every figure shown.
  • A 3D showroom built from code with three.js: each car modeled by hand from its own silhouette and sized from its published dimensions, with orbit controls and spec hotspots.
  • Explanations generated from the cars being compared, so they quote this comparison’s own numbers.
  • Unit conversions checked by automated tests against reference values (60 mph = 96.6 km/h, 300 hp = 224 kW).
  • Shareable state: the address bar always links to the exact comparison on screen.
