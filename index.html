Method and sources

What is counted, what is left out, and where every default comes from.

  • Total cost = purchase price − incentives + (energy + maintenance) × distance. These are straight lines, so the break-even distance is where two of them cross.
  • Energy uses EPA combined ratings: fuel price ÷ mpg, or electricity price × kWh per mile (measured at the wall, so charging losses are included).
  • CO2 is 8,887 g per gallon burned for gasoline, and kWh per mile × the local grid’s lifecycle intensity for electricity. Battery manufacturing adds 60 kg per kWh of pack capacity up front.
  • Left out: insurance, registration, financing, depreciation and resale, fuel-refining emissions, and building the rest of the car. Each is either buyer-specific or similar across powertrains.
  • Sourced figures were read from the linked document. Illustrative figures are stated assumptions you should replace with your own.

Engineering notes

Four problems from building this, and what each one measured out to.

18 → 9 cars, 45/45 figures sourced

Zero blanks without guessing

The first lineup had 18 US-market cars, but most makers don’t publish top speed and several don’t publish 0–60. Instead of filling gaps with simulated numbers, the lineup was cut to cars whose makers publish all five figures, and the test suite now fails on any blank.

8.90 lb/hp vs Toyota’s published 8.89

Conversions checked against the maker’s own math

All data is stored in published US units and converted in one module. Tests pin the reference values (60 mph = 96.6 km/h, 300 hp = 224 kW) and cross-check derived figures against numbers Toyota prints itself, like the GR Supra’s weight-to-power ratio.

135 MB → 19 MB for 6 real car models

Real car models that load on a phone

Two passes of cars built in code never looked like the real ones, so each is now a licensed Sketchfab model, compressed with meshopt geometry and WebP textures, then turned and scaled in code to the maker’s published length. Mesh simplification dented the GR Corolla’s body panels, so it was switched off. Only the cars on screen are downloaded.

0 px horizontal overflow at 375 px

A 3D stage that doesn’t trap your thumb

A full-height WebGL canvas swallowed touch scrolling on phones, so rotation is opt-in on touch screens. The render loop pauses off-screen, and the gauge needle sweep moved from requestAnimationFrame to timers after it stalled in background tabs.

Designed & built by Ayman Khayat

I’m Ayman Khayat, a mechanical engineering student. During an internship at a Toyota dealership I worked on hybrid powertrain diagnostics, which is where the hybrid write-up on this page comes from: regenerative braking, e-CVT behavior and battery management are what you see from the service bay, not from the brochure.

Spec sheets tell you what a car makes. They rarely tell you why two cars with similar numbers drive so differently. I built this to put the numbers next to the reasons. The Cost & Emissions mode takes on the next question: does the hybrid or the EV actually pay off, for how you drive and where you live?

Ayman on LinkedIn Source code on GitHub

What it demonstrates

  • Data integrity: every figure links to the manufacturer document it came from, and the lineup only includes cars whose makers publish every figure shown.
  • A three.js showroom with real, credited 3D car models, compressed for mobile and scaled in code to each car’s published length, with orbit controls and spec hotspots.
  • Explanations generated from the cars being compared, so they quote this comparison’s own numbers.
  • Unit conversions checked by automated tests against reference values (60 mph = 96.6 km/h, 300 hp = 224 kW).
  • A second mode, Cost & Emissions: total cost of ownership and lifetime CO2 for gas, hybrid and electric versions of the same car. It uses real fuel prices, electricity tariffs and grid carbon intensity for the US, UK, UAE and Saudi Arabia, and puts a break-even marker where the pricier option pays for itself. EVs are charged for their local grid’s emissions and their battery’s manufacturing emissions, and every default is labeled sourced or illustrative.
  • Shareable state: the address bar always links to the exact comparison on screen, in either mode.
